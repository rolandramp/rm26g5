\begin{block}{Problem}
	Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) grounds large language models in external documents by retrieving relevant passages and feeding them to the generator. The quality of a RAG pipeline depends on many interacting choices; embedding model, chunking strategy, retriever, and generator. These choices interact in ways that are difficult to predict from theory alone.

	We ask: \emph{how do different RAG configurations compare on a biomedical question-answering task, and which trade-offs between answer quality, retrieval precision, and operational cost are observed in practice?} To answer this, we systematically benchmark three RAG systems on the \texttt{rag-mini-bioasq} dataset and evaluate them with a combination of RAGAS metrics and lexical baselines.
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Benchmarked Stacks}
	All runs share the same LangChain + Chroma pipeline (Python 3.12 slim, FastAPI, Docker Compose); only generator and embedding endpoints differ. Two stacks: a self-hosted pipeline run as a 3$\times$3 factorial, and a hosted-generator pipeline run as OFAT sweeps around a baseline.

\begin{block}{Comparison Criteria \& Measurement}
	Each system is scored on the same biomedical QA test set with two complementary metric families. RAGAS uses an LLM-as-judge for the semantic metrics; BLEU and ROUGE provide a classical lexical baseline. For every metric we report \emph{min / max / mean / variance} across all questions.

	\begin{center}
	\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
	\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
	\begin{tabular}{@{}p{3.0cm} p{1.5cm} p{3.9cm}@{}}
		\toprule
		\textbf{Metric} & \textbf{Range} & \textbf{What it measures} \\
		\midrule
		\multicolumn{3}{@{}l@{}}{\textcolor{RMember}{$\blacksquare$}~\textbf{RAGAS (LLM-judged)}} \\
		Faithfulness          & $[0,1]$ & supported claims / total claims \\
		Answer correctness    & $[0,1]$ & F1 + semantic vs.\ ground truth \\
		Semantic similarity   & $[0,1]$ & cosine of answer vs.\ truth \\
		Context precision     & $[0,1]$ & ranking quality of retrieved chunks \\
		\midrule
		\multicolumn{3}{@{}l@{}}{\textcolor{RMocean}{$\blacksquare$}~\textbf{Lexical baseline}} \\
		BLEU                  & $[0,1]$ & n-gram precision \\
		ROUGE-L (F)           & $[0,1]$ & longest common subsequence overlap \\
		\bottomrule
	\end{tabular}
	\end{center}

\begin{block}{Testing Strategy}
	Same dataset, same 15 questions, same metrics --- only configuration changes between runs, so any score delta traces back to the swapped factor. A full embedding\,$\times$\,chunking grid characterises the local stack; \emph{one-factor-at-a-time} (OFAT) sweeps then probe each remaining axis around a baseline.

	\smallskip
	{\scriptsize\textbf{Per-run workload \& artefacts:} caps \texttt{max\_chunks}\,=\,4000, \texttt{max\_questions}\,=\,15; output \texttt{results/<sweep>/<level>/<timestamp>/} containing \texttt{results.json} (QA + per-question scores) and \texttt{experiment\_info.txt} (config snapshot).}
\end{block}
